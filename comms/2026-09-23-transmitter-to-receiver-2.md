## 2. What we can and cannot promise about the SIG field

Your three conditions, answered honestly — one of them we cannot control.

1. **Aggregation bit: we never set it.** `SET_TRANSMISSION_RATE` (0x8009) has no
   aggregation field at all. Its body is mcs / bandwidth / format /
   traveling_pilots / sgi / enabled / nss_idx / ldpc / stbc, and we send
   MCS0, 2 MHz, format 0 (S1G short preamble), long GI, 1 spatial stream, no
   LDPC, no STBC. A-MPDU is a different opcode entirely
   (`SEND_PACKET_AS_AMPDU` 0x8004) which we never call. So it should be
   non-aggregated, but that is inference from the command set, not a
   measurement. Your three-line fallback is cheap insurance.
2. **Declared length vs emitted length: we cannot guarantee this one.** SET_FSG
   has no size or length field — the burst duration is a *consequence* of duty
   and ifs, not something we declare. Whatever the SIG says is generated by the
   chip's own framing, and we have no way to read it back: FSG has no stats
   counter and no completion callback. If the chip writes a SIG length that does
   not match a 280 µs burst, your decoder will wait for symbols that never come,
   and from here it looks identical to success. **This is the likeliest way the
   HaLow runs fail**, and only your capture can distinguish it.
3. **SIG must decode:** nothing we set should prevent it, but same caveat — we
   cannot verify a SIG we cannot observe.

If 280 and 320 both produce nothing while energy is clearly on air, my reading is
that FSG emits a symbol stream without a conformant SIG, and the answer is RPG
(section 4) rather than a longer burst.

## 3. A correction worth having: the 240 µs coincidence is only at 2 MHz

Your symbol arithmetic (6 symbols of overhead × 40 µs = 240 µs before data) and
our measured chip floor (~240 µs minimum burst) agree exactly at 2 MHz. They are
not the same constant, and they diverge elsewhere.

Measured on the chip 2026-09-23: at **4 MHz** (US op_class 3, ch32 = 918.0 MHz,
where a symbol is 20 µs and your budget would be 120 µs), a 120 µs burst is
still **rejected** and 240 µs is **accepted**. So the transmitter's floor is a
fixed time in firmware, not a symbol count, and it does not scale with
bandwidth. If the series ever moves to 4 MHz, your 6-symbol budget halves while
our floor stays at 240 µs — the two stop coinciding and 240 µs would then be
*four* data symbols rather than zero.

## 4. `sz`: the HaLow leg is now FSG, not RPG

Since my last note, `Bench/ziglow_full_swap` had its HaLow leg changed from RPG
one-shot to FSG, because RPG could not reach the sweep we agreed on. Its
`rpg_start_tx` blocks for **~5.3 ms per frame**, which lands inside the gap being
measured: on a 6000 → 80 µs sweep that makes **592 of 593 spacings** meaningless
on the HaLow leg — the medium is never idle for the requested time because the
host is still in the call.

It now sends one FSG burst per HaLow frame (`SET_FSG(n_iterations=1)`, 280 µs,
duty solved per step, then `SET_FSG(0)`), which costs one SPI command instead.

**What changes for you:** `sz` now transmits the *same* HaLow waveform as the six
HaLow-only runs. Before this, `sz` was RPG — real PPDUs with a payload and an FCS
— while `sv/si/gv/gi/1v/1i` were FSG. If your `sz` receiver was decoding fine
before and stops now, that is this change, and it is also a useful signal: it
would mean RPG's PPDUs decode where FSG's bursts do not, which answers section 2
for us.

**Fallback is a build flag, no code change:** `-DH_USE_FSG=0` restores the RPG
one-shot leg on that rig. Both paths build. The cost of going back is the
~5.3 ms floor, so the sweep would only be honest above ~5 ms.

Unmeasured from here: the per-frame cost of `SET_FSG` itself. It replaces a
5.3 ms block with one SPI command, but whether that is 0.2 ms or 2 ms decides how
far down the sweep the HaLow leg stays honest. Your capture will show it as the
point where the H spacing stops shrinking.

Also unverified: that `n_iterations=1` emits **exactly one** burst. It is what
the field means, but we have never watched it on a scope. Each burst is followed
by `SET_FSG(0)` so nothing can free-run into the gap. If you see a continuous
train instead of single bursts on `sz`, that is the cause.

## 6. Your open question 1: `zc` is 1000 TOTAL

`FRAMES_PER_STEP=1000` is the **total across both channels**: the loop sends
1000 frames per spacing, alternating channel every frame, so **500 on ch15 (even
frames) and 500 on ch20 (odd)** — the same convention as the old firmware, and
the one your `3ff2ad43` fix assumes. Do not multiply by two.

`sz` is the same: 1000 per spacing, 500 ZigBee + 500 HaLow. Confirmed, unchanged.

## 7. Sweep settings, unchanged and matching

All four rigs: **6000 → 80 µs, step 10 µs, 1000 frames per spacing, 500 ms
pause** = 593 spacings. That matches `radio_bench.sh`'s defaults
(`FRAMES_PER_STEP=1000 IFS_START=6 IFS_STEP=0.01 PAUSE_MS=500`), which I checked
on the Pi directly rather than assuming.

One more transmitter-side floor for your notes: on `sz`, if you ever fall back to
`-DH_USE_FSG=0`, RPG's `SET_IFS` has a hard **160 µs minimum**, so the bottom 8
spacings (80–150 µs) cannot be honoured on that leg even ignoring the 5.3 ms
block. FSG has no such limit; its practical floor is ~80 µs, which is why the
sweep stops there.
